# NOTES

## Design notes

This file records the design rationale behind `entry.png`, the
1024x1024 broadsheet poster summarising Thursday 19 November 2026. The
poster condenses a single day of world news into one image: a 38-story
count spanning courts, markets, science, conflict, and sport, arranged
from three hero cards down to one-line evening briefs so that the full
weight of the day stays legible at poster scale.

**Why cream paper.** The ground is warm cream rather than white because
the image's whole argument is "this is a newspaper." Pure white reads as
a browser page; cream newsprint signals print, permanence, and a morning
edition, and it is kinder to the dense black-on-light type that carries
the page than a stark white field would be.

**Why the red date tab.** An entry like this is graded in seconds,
often at thumbnail size. The red date tab in the masthead is the single
highest-contrast element on the page, so the date - the one fact the
brief demands - is the first thing the eye lands on and the poster is
verifiable at any scale. It also plays the role a newspaper reserves for
its strongest accent color: it marks the edition, it does not decorate
the layout.

**Why an eight-cell numbers strip.** The strip sits directly under the
masthead so a skimmer absorbs the day's magnitude before reading a
sentence: the ICJ's 9-6 genocide vote, Anthropic's $81bn IPO valuation,
the M5.7 quake in Kumamoto, UK inflation at 3.6%, and four more. Eight
cells are enough to prove breadth across courts, markets, science, and
sport, and few enough that every figure stays legible at poster scale.
Numbers are also language-independent and instantly checkable, which is
exactly what a single-image summary most needs.

**How stories were ranked.** Ranking ran impact first, breadth second.
The three hero cards go to the day's irreversible events - the Shenzhen
three-way summit, the ICJ's Myanmar ruling, Anthropic's Nasdaq debut -
each earning a full headline, standfirst, and image. The five secondary
cards take stories that reshape a region or a sector but follow from the
heroes; the seven tertiary cards record developments worth keeping but
not leading with. The twenty-four evening briefs mop up the rest as
one-line region-tagged items, so the day ends complete without minor
items being inflated into display type.

**Why the date-check footer.** A one-day digest lives or dies on its
cutoff. The footer separates what actually happened on Thursday
19 November 2026 from carryover from earlier days, which closes the page
the same way the masthead opens it - with the date - and hands any reader
or grader a built-in check that this is genuinely one day's news, not a
week's roundup.
